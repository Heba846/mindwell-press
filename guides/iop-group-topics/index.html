IOP group topics: 78 session ideas for adult mental health IOP and PHP groups

By Heba El Garhy · Published October 25, 2026 · Editorial standards

Running groups five days a week uses up topics fast, and the usual lists online mix icebreaker games with substance-use worksheets. These are 78 group topics for adult mental health IOP and PHP programs, sorted by group type: process, skills, psychoeducation, and extra sessions for PHP. Each one stands on its own, so it works in a group where members join on different days.

How to choose today's topic

  1. Check who is in the room. If someone is on their first day, pick a topic that needs no earlier session, and plan a two-minute orientation before you start.
  2. Balance the day. Pair a teaching group, skills or psychoeducation, with a process group, so members both learn something and use it with each other.
  3. Follow the room, within limits. If the group keeps raising sleep or loneliness, move that topic up the rotation. Don't rebuild the day around one member's crisis; handle that through your program's procedure.
  4. Plan two depths. Have one reflection question for first-time members and one for people who have heard the skill before. Returning members disengage when every session is pitched at day one.

The codes next to each topic match the session codes in Volume 1 of the Open-Enrollment Program Series, where each one is a full session plan. You don't need the book to use this list.

Process group topics (18)

A process group gives members room to talk about what is happening for them now, with each other. The topic is a starting point, not a lesson plan. Each of these works on its own, so a first-day member can take part without having heard the last one.

  1. Arriving PR-01
    What it is like to be here today, for a first-day member and a returning one alike.
  2. Carrying the Week PR-02
    What members brought in with them, and what they can set down for the hour.
  3. The Mask and the Mirror PR-03
    The gap between how members come across and how they feel inside.
  4. Frustration and Stuckness PR-04
    Where progress feels slow, and what being stuck looks like day to day.
  5. Trust and Safety Here PR-05
    What makes it easier or harder to speak honestly in this room.
  6. The Inner Critic PR-06
    The judging voice, where it learned its lines, and how members answer it.
  7. Grief, Loss, and What Changed PR-07
    Losses of people, roles, health or plans, and what changed afterward.
  8. Hope and Doubt PR-08
    Holding both at once without having to settle which one wins.
  9. Connection and Isolation PR-09
    Pulling away, reaching out, and what gets in the way of each.
  10. Resilience and Quiet Wins PR-10
    Small things that went better, which members tend to skip past.
  11. Giving and Receiving Feedback PR-11
    Practicing honest, kind feedback with each other in real time.
  12. Anger and Boundaries PR-12
    What anger is pointing to, and where a limit may be needed.
  13. Shame and Self-Forgiveness PR-13
    What shame makes people hide, and what easing self-blame could look like.
  14. Identity Beyond the Diagnosis PR-14
    Who members are apart from symptoms, labels and treatment.
  15. Navigating Outside Relationships PR-15
    Family, partners, friends and work while someone is in treatment.
  16. Setbacks and Recovery Momentum PR-16
    Hard days and lapses, and how members keep going after them.
  17. Preparing for Transition / Stepping Down PR-17
    Feelings and plans as discharge or a lower level of care gets close.
  18. Endings, Goodbyes, and Continuity PR-18
    Saying goodbye well, for the member who leaves and the group that stays.

Skills group topics (18)

Skills groups teach one concrete skill, practice it in the room and plan one use before the next group. The first three modules follow Linehan's DBT skill areas. The rest draw on cognitive behavioral therapy, acceptance and commitment therapy and behavioral activation.

Mindfulness

  1. Observe and Describe SK-MF-01
    Noticing experience and putting it into words without judging it.
  2. Grounding When Overwhelmed SK-MF-02
    Using the senses and the body to come back to the present moment.
  3. Attention Training SK-MF-03
    Practicing where attention goes, and bringing it back when it drifts.

Emotion regulation

  1. Emotions, Urges, and Actions SK-ER-01
    How a feeling, the urge it brings and the action that follows connect.
  2. Check the Facts and Opposite Action SK-ER-02
    Testing whether an emotion fits the facts, and acting opposite when it does not help.
  3. Reducing Emotional Vulnerability SK-ER-03
    Sleep, food, movement and routine as the base for steadier moods.

Distress tolerance

  1. Crisis Survival Skills SK-DT-01
    Getting through an intense moment without making the situation worse.
  2. Radical Acceptance and Turning the Mind SK-DT-02
    Accepting what cannot be changed right now, and choosing acceptance again.
  3. Self-Soothing with the Senses and a Distraction Plan SK-DT-03
    A personal list of what calms and what distracts, ready before it is needed.

Thoughts (CBT and ACT)

  1. The Thought-Feeling-Behavior Connection and Common Thinking Traps SK-CG-01
    The CBT triangle and the thinking patterns that keep low mood and anxiety going.
  2. Examining the Evidence and Thought Reframing SK-CG-02
    Weighing a thought against the facts and finding a more balanced version.
  3. Cognitive Defusion and Noticing Hooked Thoughts SK-CG-03
    Stepping back from a thought instead of arguing with it.

Behavioral activation

  1. Activity Scheduling and the Pleasure-Mastery Balance SK-BA-01
    Planning activities that bring enjoyment or a sense of achievement.
  2. Values-Based Action and Overcoming Avoidance SK-BA-02
    Choosing actions by what matters, even when avoidance pulls the other way.
  3. Graded Task Assignment and Habit Stacking SK-BA-03
    Breaking tasks into small steps and attaching new habits to existing ones.

Interpersonal effectiveness

  1. Objective Effectiveness and Assertive Communication SK-IE-01
    Asking clearly for what you need.
  2. Relationship Effectiveness and Boundary Setting SK-IE-02
    Holding a limit while keeping the relationship.
  3. Self-Respect Effectiveness and Saying No SK-IE-03
    Responding to others in a way that fits your own values.

Psychoeducation group topics (18)

Psychoeducation explains what members are experiencing and why, in plain language. Keep it interactive: a short teaching point, a question to the group, and one way to apply it. A lecture loses a room fast.

  1. Understanding Depression PE-01
    Common signs, and how depression affects energy, thinking and activity.
  2. Understanding Anxiety and the Fight-or-Flight Response PE-02
    The body's alarm system, and why anxiety is felt so strongly in the body.
  3. Chronic Stress and Recovery Time PE-03
    How ongoing stress builds up, and why recovery time matters.
  4. The Panic Cycle and Interoceptive Conditioning PE-04
    How body sensations and fear feed each other during panic.
  5. Sleep and Mood PE-05
    How sleep and mood affect each other, and habits that support sleep.
  6. Strong Emotions and Making Room to Pause PE-06
    What happens in the moment of a strong emotion, and how to create a pause.
  7. The Window of Tolerance and Autonomic Regulation PE-07
    The zone where people can think and feel at the same time, and signs of leaving it.
  8. Understanding Trauma, Triggers, and the Nervous System PE-08
    How triggers work, taught without asking anyone to share their story.
  9. Shame, Self-Criticism, and Self-Compassion PE-09
    Guilt versus shame, and what self-compassion is and is not.
  10. Values, Meaning, and Personal Direction PE-10
    Naming personal values and using them as a guide for decisions.
  11. Understanding Anger and Emotional Triggers as Functional Signals PE-11
    Anger as information about needs, limits and what feels unfair.
  12. Grief, Loss, and Non-Death Transitions PE-12
    Grief after a death and after other losses, such as a job, health or a relationship.
  13. Loneliness and Meaningful Connection PE-13
    Being alone versus feeling lonely, and small ways to build connection.
  14. Interpersonal Boundaries and Relationship Systems PE-14
    What boundaries are, and how relationship patterns repeat.
  15. Executive Dysfunction, Task Paralysis, and Motivation PE-15
    Why starting a task can feel impossible, and why waiting for motivation often backfires.
  16. Building Resilient Support Networks and Crisis Navigation PE-16
    Mapping who and what helps, and which resource to use when.
  17. Relapse Prevention: Lapses, Relapses, and Recovery Curves PE-17
    The difference between a lapse and a relapse, and why recovery is rarely a straight line.
  18. Stepping Down: Maintenance, Care Continuity, and Generalization PE-18
    Keeping skills going after the program and planning the next level of care.

Extra topics for PHP (24)

Partial hospitalization days are longer, so programs need more groups. These cover daily structure, safety and relapse planning, the move to a lower level of care, and practice in the room.

Safety-planning topics. Run the safety and relapse planning sessions only within your program's risk procedures, with the staff your program requires. A group topic is not a risk assessment.

Daily routines and wellness

  1. Daily Rhythm Anchors PH-WS-01
    Fixed points that give the day a shape.
  2. Eating Routines, Access, and Daily Energy PH-WS-02
    Regular meals, practical barriers to them, and their effect on energy.
  3. Building an Evening Wind-Down PH-WS-03
    A short, repeatable routine for the end of the day.
  4. Daily Demands and Energy Pacing PH-WS-04
    Matching the day's demands to the energy available.
  5. Medication Adherence and Prescriber Partnership PH-WS-05
    Questions and barriers to bring to a prescriber. The group gives no medication advice.
  6. Rest, Withdrawal, and What I Need PH-WS-06
    Telling restful time apart from pulling away from people.

Safety and relapse planning

  1. Deconstructing the Crisis Trajectory: Micro-Triggers and Prodromal Signs PH-SP-01
    The early warning signs that tend to come before a crisis.
  2. Internal Coping vs. Social Distraction PH-SP-02
    When to use a skill on your own, and when to reach for people.
  3. Environmental Safety and Means Restriction PH-SP-03
    Making surroundings safer during high-risk periods, within program protocol.
  4. The Anatomy of an Urge: Urge Surfing and Cognitive Decoupling PH-SP-04
    Riding out an urge without acting on it.
  5. Post-Lapse Cognitive Processing: Reducing the Abstinence Violation Effect PH-SP-05
    Responding to a lapse without the all-or-nothing thinking that turns it into a relapse.
  6. Reviewing Safety-Plan Access, Contacts, and Gaps PH-SP-06
    Checking that each member's plan is current, reachable and complete.

Transition and stepping down

  1. Questions About Levels of Care and Stepping Down PH-TS-01
    What changes between PHP, IOP and outpatient care.
  2. Roles and Identity Beyond Treatment PH-TS-02
    Returning to roles at home, work or study after a program.
  3. My Routine and Check-In Plan PH-TS-03
    A weekly structure and who checks in with whom.
  4. Outpatient Follow-Up and Handoff Questions PH-TS-04
    What to ask before the first outpatient appointment.
  5. Mixed Feelings About Leaving PH-TS-05
    Relief, worry and loss about leaving the program, all at once.
  6. My Recovery Reference PH-TS-06
    A one-page summary of what has helped, to keep after discharge.

Practice in the room

  1. Practicing a Request or Boundary PH-AP-01
    Rehearsing a real request or limit with the group.
  2. Noticing Mild Body Sensations and Grounding PH-AP-02
    Low-intensity practice in noticing sensations and grounding.
  3. Working With a Manageable Current Thought PH-AP-03

  4. Taking a First Step in the Room PH-AP-04

Building a week from these lists

Most programs rotate through topics rather than running them in a fixed order, because members arrive and leave on different days. A simple way to build a rotation:

  • Run the skills modules as blocks. Three sessions per module, one module at a time, so a member who stays for the full program meets every skill area.
  • Spread the process topics. Keep transition and ending topics (17 and 18 above) for weeks when someone is stepping down.
  • Fit psychoeducation to the census. If several members are working on panic or sleep, bring those topics forward.
  • Plan for low attendance and holidays. Decide in advance what runs when only two members show up, so the session still counts.

Common questions

What is the difference between process, skills and psychoeducation groups?

A skills group teaches and practices one concrete skill. A psychoeducation group explains a condition or process, such as anxiety or sleep, in plain language. A process group gives members room to talk with each other about what is happening for them now, with the facilitator guiding the conversation rather than teaching.

How many groups does an IOP or PHP day usually include?

It varies by program and payer. IOP days are commonly about three hours, often two or three groups, while PHP days are longer and need more groups. That is why PHP programs need a larger topic list.

Can I use these topics in a rolling-admission group?

Yes. Every topic on this list is written to stand alone, so it works when new members join on any day. Give each session a short orientation for first-day members and separate reflection questions for first-time and returning members.

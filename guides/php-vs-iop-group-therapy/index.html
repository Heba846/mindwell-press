PHP vs IOP group therapy: what changes for the facilitator

By Heba El Garhy · Published November 2, 2026 · Updated November 9, 2026 · Editorial standards

The two programs use the same group formats, but PHP asks you to fill far more hours. If you are moving from an IOP to a PHP, or building a curriculum for both, the differences that matter are time, volume and how many groups a member attends in a day.

Hours and structure

The difference between the two levels of care is mostly intensity. The Medicare Benefit Policy Manual sets the thresholds directly: it requires a plan of care evidencing the need for a minimum of 20 hours per week of therapeutic services for partial hospitalization (PHP, chapter 6 §70.3) and a minimum of 9 hours per week of services for intensive outpatient care (IOP, chapter 6 §70.4). Medicare itself describes a partial hospitalization program as a structured outpatient program that serves as an alternative to inpatient psychiatric care, usually involving about four to eight hours of care per day. State definitions of adult IOP use similar thresholds. For example, Nebraska's definition sets nine or more hours of skilled treatment per week across three to five days.

Programs, states and payers set their own rules, and they change. Check yours before you plan a schedule around these numbers.

Side by side

Weekly hoursIOP: a minimum of 9 hours per week (Medicare Benefit Policy Manual ch. 6 §70.4; Nebraska's adult definition sets a similar threshold). PHP: a minimum of 20 hours per week of therapeutic services (Medicare Benefit Policy Manual ch. 6 §70.3).
Typical dayIOP: commonly about three hours, often two or three groups, three or four days a week. PHP: several hours a day, so more groups per day.
PurposeIOP: structured care that lets members keep up daily life. PHP: framed by Medicare as an alternative to inpatient care.
Medicare certificationIOP: no inpatient-alternative certification required. PHP: a physician or qualified professional must certify the member would otherwise need inpatient care.

What changes for the facilitator

  1. You need more sessions. A day with more groups burns through a topic list quickly. A list built for a three-hour IOP day will run out first in a PHP.
  2. You repeat yourself more carefully. A member who attends daily will hear the same skill from different facilitators. Plan the rotation so repeats are deliberate, with a harder application the second time. When a member moves between levels, see how to handle a member stepping down mid-rotation.
  3. You balance the day. Pair a teaching group, skills or psychoeducation, with a process group, so members both learn something and use it with each other.
  4. You document more often. More groups per day means more notes per member. A structure you can write quickly and consistently matters more.

Documentation

The note formats are the same at both levels. What changes is volume, so write in a format your team can complete between groups. See GIRP vs DAP vs BIRP vs SOAP for group therapy and Group therapy progress notes.

What this comparison does not decide

Which level of care a person needs is a clinical decision made through your program's assessment and treatment planning, along with payer rules. This guide covers how the groups differ, not who should be in which program.

Common questions

What is the difference between PHP and IOP group therapy?

The main difference is intensity. A Medicare contractor's comparison puts PHP at at least 20 hours of therapeutic services per week and IOP at at least 9 hours. PHP days are longer and need more groups, so facilitators need a larger topic list. Your program and payer set the exact requirements.

How many hours a week is an IOP compared with a PHP?

Medicare sources give at least 9 hours a week for IOP and at least 20 hours a week for PHP. State definitions of adult IOP use similar numbers; Nebraska's, for example, sets nine or more hours across three to five days. Programs and payers set their own rules, so confirm yours.

Do I need different group topics for PHP than for IOP?

You need more of them. The same group formats work at both levels, but a PHP day contains more groups, so a topic list sized for a three-hour IOP day runs out sooner. Standalone sessions that any member can join on any day make the extra hours easier to plan.

Does the note format change between PHP and IOP?

No. GIRP, DAP, BIRP and SOAP all work at both levels. What changes is volume, because a PHP member attends more groups a day. Choose a format your team can complete between groups, and follow your program's documentation policy and payer rules.
